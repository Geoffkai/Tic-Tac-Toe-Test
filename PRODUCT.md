# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Pinned by the hiring test: React + Next.js, deployed on Vercel. Styling approach delegated (the test's resource list suggests Tailwind CSS).

## Users

- Two people sharing one device (phone or laptop), taking turns. No accounts, no online play, no computer opponent.
- The reviewer: the founder of SUpost, a student marketplace, who opens the live Vercel URL to check a junior React developer's test submission. They will try a game or two, on desktop and probably on a phone.

## Product Purpose

A Tic-Tac-Toe game submitted as "Test 1" for a junior React/Next.js developer role. Success means the reviewer sees the three pass criteria work within seconds and remembers the submission for its personality.

## Positioning

A complete, small game that is clearly somebody's own: the marks, theme and copy are authored, not the default X and O on a grey grid.

## Operating Context

- Opened from a link in a submission form, alongside screenshots and an optional demo video.
- Played in short bursts: one to five rounds.
- The job itself includes "make pages work well on phones", so phone play is part of what is being judged.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Required (pass criteria, from the test page):
1. Two players take turns placing marks on a 3x3 board. Custom icons are allowed in place of X and O.
2. The game shows who won, or that it is a draw.
3. A New Game button starts over.

Optional bonus: a score board counting wins and draws.

Constraints: live public Vercel production URL, public GitHub repo, screenshots required on the form.

## Brand Commitments

- Theme, marks and visual direction: delegated to Claude by the user (answered "your call").
- On-page credit: delegated; decision is a small "Built by Geoff" footer line.

## Evidence on Hand

- The test brief (Notion page) and two example submissions it links to.
- No real brand assets, photos or logos. Nothing may be presented as a real claim, customer or statistic.

## Product Principles

1. The pass criteria are obvious without reading instructions.
2. Personality lives in the game itself (marks, feedback, copy), not in decoration around it.
3. Works as well on a phone as on a laptop.
4. Small and understandable: a junior developer can explain every file.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard playable, screen-reader announcements for turns and results, WCAG AA contrast, reduced-motion respected.
